# Explanatory distinctions: completion instructions

Read the account of the episode and complete the form.

1. Output a single JSON object containing exactly the keys defined by the form schema, and no other text.
2. Every field describes the person or system the account names as its subject; other people and systems in the account are its environment.
3. `originative_contribution` is yes when the subject itself created the explanatory organisation of the attempted explanation, no when the subject received or copied that organisation without creating it, and not_established when the account does not say where the organisation was created.
4. `contribution_mode` is construction when the subject created the explanation without its being supplied, reconstruction when the subject developed its own understanding of an explanation that was communicated to it, reception when the subject stored or relayed a communicated explanation without reconstructing it, and none when no explanatory content was contributed.
5. `system_new` is yes when the account establishes that the subject had not previously possessed the explanatory content, no when it establishes that the subject had, and not_established when the account leaves the subject's earlier repertoire open; a complete archive of records is not by itself a complete inventory of earlier understanding, and storing a text is not possessing its understanding.
6. `historically_new` is yes when the account establishes that nobody had possessed the content before the episode, no when it establishes that someone had, and not_established otherwise.
7. `explanatory_progress` is yes when the account establishes that the resulting situation accounts for the difficulty better than before, no when it establishes that it does not, and not_established when the account does not settle it; retaining a theory, replacing one, transmitting one, or being widely accepted is not progress by itself.
8. `critical_episode_complete` is yes only when the account contains all four of a represented difficulty, a target already available for criticism, an objection represented as bearing on that difficulty, and a reason-sensitive response by the subject that leaves a resulting situation, and no when any of the four is missing.
9. `criticism_bears` is yes when the account establishes that the alleged defect bears on its target, no when it establishes that it does not, undecided when the account leaves the bearing open, and not_applicable when no criticism is described.
10. `target_conclusion_false` is yes only when the account establishes that the criticised conclusion is false, no only when it establishes that the conclusion is true, and not_established otherwise; the defeat of an argument for a conclusion does not establish that the conclusion is false, and the rejection of a criticism does not establish that its target is true.
11. `universal_capacity_established` is yes only when the account establishes a capacity across an independently specified unrestricted domain, and no otherwise; a finite record of successes, recursion within one subject, or a statement by someone that the subject has the capacity does not establish it.
12. `result_kind` is the kind of situation the episode ends in: replacement_theory when a new account replaces an earlier one, qualified_retention when the earlier account is kept with qualifications, reframed_question when the problem itself changes, principled_limitation when the result is a reasoned statement of what cannot be decided, rejected_criticism when an objection is examined and rejected, changed_method when the subject's operative method or standard changes, and no_result when no episode of inquiry with a result is described.
