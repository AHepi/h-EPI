Harbor Relay Charter HRC-1
Synthetic qualification authority — version 1

Status and use

This is a fictional procedural charter written only to qualify a translation
harness. It describes no real harbour, organization, or practice. Only the
numbered clauses and their footnotes are rule-bearing. Headings and the example
aid reading but create no rule unless a numbered clause says otherwise.

1. Boundary

This charter governs whether one relay round is procedurally closed. It says
nothing about ownership, the truth of a record, employee competence, or what
makes two contents incompatible.

2. Opening

A round begins when its coordinator enters OPEN after at least one record has
been registered in that round. Registration without OPEN does not begin the
round.

3. Offer role

A record is an OFFER in a round when an entry event assigns it as the proposed
manifest after OPEN. Byte-identical records need not have the same role in
different rounds.

4. Challenge role

A record is a CHALLENGE to an OFFER exactly when it is entered after that
offer, names it, cites a constraint registered before it, and states an
incompatibility with that constraint.

5. Overlap and priority

One record may challenge an earlier offer and simultaneously offer its
replacement. No challenge can target a record that has not already become an
offer.

6. Responses

KEEP, REPLACE, WITHDRAW, and PAUSE are the response kinds. PAUSE is a response
but leaves the named challenge live. Each other response answers only the
challenge it names.

7. Eligibility

A replacement is eligible exactly when it addresses every incompatibility
stated in its named challenge and leaves all unnamed entries unchanged.
Eligibility does not imply entry, selection, or acceptance.

8. Ordinary closure

An ordinary round closes when a countersigned CLOSE is entered, at least one
offer remains active, and every live challenge has received a response other
than PAUSE. Satisfying those conditions without entering CLOSE does not close
the round.

9. Emergency exception

An EMERGENCY-SEAL is the only exception to clause 8. It may close a round
containing paused challenges exactly when it names every such challenge and an
independent reviewer counter-signs it.¹ The seal does not convert PAUSE or
answer a challenge.

10. Order

Only ordering relations stated by these clauses are supplied. Other events may
be concurrent; the charter supplies no total order.

11. Availability

A record is available for later inspection exactly while it is retrievable
from the registry. Availability does not imply actual inspection, use, or
endorsement.

12. Open predicates

The charter supplies no rule deciding content identity, correctness of an
incompatibility claim, or reviewer independence. These remain inputs rather
than definitions.

13. Unresolved reference

After a reviewer inspects a CHALLENGE and counter-signs the EMERGENCY-SEAL that
cites it, the record may be copied into the next round.

14. Example

Example only: in round R, record A becomes an OFFER; record B later becomes a
CHALLENGE to A; record C becomes both a response to B and a replacement OFFER;
the coordinator then enters CLOSE. This example neither exhausts nor extends
the numbered rules.

¹ The charter deliberately supplies no test of reviewer independence; clause
12 therefore governs that input. The line break in “counter-signs” is ordinary
text layout and does not create a second operation.
