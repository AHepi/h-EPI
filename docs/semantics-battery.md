# The semantics battery: five models on the labelling policy and the explanatory distinctions

*What five models of five sizes did when asked to do the two things the semantics behind this harness's appraisal layer makes precise: compute the labelling policy's least fixed point over a dossier of arguments, and classify an episode of inquiry under the distinctions the semantics keeps apart. Two pilots, one run each per model, conjectures written before the runs. Nothing here says a model understands argumentation or explanation; the records say what each returned.*

## Why these two tasks

The semantics the appraisal was drawn from (`docs/reports/provable-in-the-semantics.md`) has one decidable core and one body of distinctions. The core is the labelling policy: given arguments with a readiness, essential dependencies, and attacks, apply two rules to a fixed point and read off in, out, and undecided. It is grounded semantics with necessary support, its least-fixed-point property is checked by this harness's offline suite on every two-node graph, and the harness's own `appraise` computes it. The distinctions are the identifications the semantics forbids: authorship from transmission, newness from a complete archive, progress from retention or replacement, falsehood of a conclusion from the defeat of an argument for it, truth from a rejected criticism, universal capacity from a finite record or from recursion inside one subject.

Both are document-to-form tasks, so neither needed a change to the machine beyond one configuration surface (a rival reading that fixes several fields, described in `docs/how-it-works.md`). The first has an answer key computed by code; the second has an answer key stipulated by the semantics, marked provisional wherever the semantics itself leaves the reading open.

## The two pilots

**`appraisal-labelling`.** Eighteen dossiers of six arguments A1 to A6, each with a readiness of PASS, FAIL, or UNKNOWN, the arguments it depends on essentially, and the arguments it attacks, plus zero to three attached cases with a polarity. Twelve are designed to exercise one structure each: a support chain broken by an attack (LAB-01), a mutual attack resolved from outside (LAB-02), a support cycle under an unknown attacker (LAB-03), reinstatement through a criticism of a criticism (LAB-04), no edges with everything PASS (LAB-05) or everything UNKNOWN (LAB-06), a self-attack (LAB-07), an odd attack cycle broken from outside (LAB-08), an unknown root with a chain above it (LAB-09), cases on in, out, and dependent-of-out arguments (LAB-10), cases only on losing arguments (LAB-11), and a failed root with a chain of dependents (LAB-12). Six more are seeded random slices. The form is the label of each argument, the count of arguments in, and the raw and usable case summaries. The instructions state the two rules and the fixed-point procedure in three sentences and nothing else about the policy. Every key comes from `appraise`, and a test checks every case against it. The rival rule appended by the RIVAL_SUBSTITUTION family says that UNKNOWN counts as PASS, or that it never does; the keys for every label that moves under the first rule are carried with it. Twins exchange the positions of the first two label fields; controls swap them, drop the count, and add a policy key; repeats are two per baseline; the table rendering carries the same dossier as rows.

**`explanatory-distinctions`.** Twelve episodes with a named subject, each built from one of the semantics' discriminating cases: a relay that republishes an explanation (D-01) and the analyst who later reconstructs it (D-02); an inherited theory criticised with an original explanation (D-03); a criticism whose stated grounds are withdrawn (D-04); a supplied explanation reconstructed by a learner whose tutor then claims he can originate (D-05); the two balances (D-06); a copier of a good explanation (D-07); a group that made one contribution and then ceased (D-08); a system that criticises its own standards inside one subject and cannot represent a problem outside it (D-09); a novel attempted explanation later shown false (D-10); an objection examined and rejected with reasons (D-11); and a prover with forty successes in two areas whose developers call it universal (D-12). The form is ten classifications: originative contribution, mode of contribution, newness to the subject, newness to everyone, explanatory progress, whether a complete critical episode is described, whether the criticism bears, whether the target conclusion is established false, whether a universal capacity is established, and the kind of result. Every field but two has a not_established or not_applicable option. The instructions define each field in one sentence and state, in the sentence that defines it, the identification the semantics forbids. Where the semantics leaves a reading open the key admits every reading it admits and is marked provisional; the trap fields (a relay's authorship, a reconstruction's historical newness, falsehood from defeat, truth from a rejected criticism, capacity from a record) are exact. The rival rule says that reconstructing a supplied explanation counts as an originative contribution, or that only an unsupplied one does. Twins exchange the two newness fields; controls swap them, drop the result kind, and add a confidence key.

## The five models

Five models of five sizes served by the same endpoint, temperature zero, seed seven, thinking off in the request: gpt-oss:20b (a 21B mixture with about 3.6B active parameters), gemma4:31b (31B dense), gpt-oss:120b (117B mixture, about 5B active), qwen3.5:397b, and mistral-large-3:675b. They were chosen to span the size ladder, not as representatives of anything, and the two gpt-oss models are the only family pair.

## The conjectures, written first

Both pilots carry a `claims.json` committed before any run (`c7f6548`): twenty-three conjectures about the labelling task (LAB-01 to LAB-20 and three structural ones) and twenty-two about the distinctions (DIS-01 to DIS-19 and three structural ones). They were written from the semantics, not from records, so a refutation below tests a prediction. The tables of their outcomes are generated by `claims` and are the first thing to read.

- **S1. The two models that reasoned before answering computed the fixed point; the three that did not, did not, whatever their size.** gpt-oss:120b labelled every baseline dossier as the harness does; gpt-oss:20b, a 21B mixture with about 3.6B active parameters, missed one. gemma4:31b, qwen3.5:397b, and mistral-large-3:675b, which answered in about a hundred tokens, labelled 7, 8, and 9 of 18 dossiers as the harness does, and their misses fall on the structures the rules must be iterated to resolve: the support cycle under an unknown attacker (LAB-03: `a66e00d2f21a126d`, `ff9b2046ddeec41f`, `a0d6256fc832f442`), reinstatement (LAB-04), the odd cycle (LAB-08), the self-attack (LAB-07). Between 31B dense and 675B there is no ordering in these counts; between a hundred tokens and a thousand there is.
- **S2. The self-attack is where the reasoning model broke.** gpt-oss:20b labelled the self-attacking A1 undecided, as the policy does, but then left A2 and A3 undecided too (`dcc7033bffce6cd0`), where A3, attacked by nothing, is in and A2, attacked by A3, is out whatever A1's label. The three non-reasoning models put A1 out (`ce78624cc9d77024`, `974ea46e05f051d2`, `1f4373cd886169c1`), which is the natural reading of "attacks itself" and not the policy's.
- **S3. A rule that restated the baseline broke the non-reasoning models.** The appended sentence "an argument whose readiness is UNKNOWN is never in" changes no label, since sentence 3 already requires PASS. Under it gemma4:31b matched the key's count on none of the seven rival dossiers and mistral and qwen on one each; on the all-unknown dossier gemma and mistral labelled every argument out (`f0449264ba840da9`, `23818085b444c2e8`), reading "never in" as "out". gpt-oss:120b matched all seven under both rules; gpt-oss:20b four and five. The rule that does change labels, UNKNOWN as ready, was matched by the larger reasoning model on all seven dossiers and by every other model on three or four.
- **S4. Rendering the dossier as a table hurt the smaller reasoning model most.** Six of gpt-oss:20b's sixteen table renderings carried a criticism its prose baseline did not (`0eb4bdeae5933bfd`, `c2253969c47d5e33`, `baaa1e1a299eeb2b`), and on one it labelled all six arguments undecided (`e844e0393f7f20e1`, LAB-R03, where four are in and two out). gemma4:31b's table renderings added no criticism at all, which for a model wrong on eleven prose baselines means the table did not change what it did. LAB-14, that prose and table are labelled the same way, is refuted by four models and not by gemma.
- **S5. The larger reasoning model's only errors were on repeats.** gpt-oss:120b's 18 of 18 is a one-sample fact: six of its thirty-two byte-identical repeats returned different labels, and every one of its label criticisms in the run is on a repeat or a table rendering (`c4935faf8ba80dbf`, `b7c01b0448028f77` on LAB-03; `90029b9b4492aed4` on LAB-01; `28dc7dc9b8fc438e` on LAB-07). The same request that was labelled as the harness labels it was, one call later, labelled otherwise. LAB-16 is refuted by all five models.
- **S6. Removing the procedure sentence changed nothing for the model that already computes the fixed point.** With sentence 5 (apply the two rules to a fixed point) removed, gpt-oss:120b's output was identical to its baseline on all sixteen dossiers (`0393e42c571e7b34`, `06142ded50a0c8db`); with sentence 4 (the out rule) removed it changed on eight (`099bb9826b24ffdb`). For gemma4:31b the removals changed three and four of sixteen (`1be46db731614adf`, `7d86f21685c46c54`), from a baseline that was mostly wrong. The dependence family records this and judges nothing; the reading offered is that the larger model was not following sentence 5 but doing what sentence 5 describes, which is consistent with knowing grounded semantics and is not established by it. LAB-17, that removing either sentence always changes the output, is refuted by all five.
- **S7. Reasoning length was neither necessary nor sufficient.** LAB-18, that a reply of 800 tokens or more never mislabels, is refuted by both reasoning models (17 observations, mostly S4 and S5); LAB-19, that a reply under 400 tokens never labels a random dossier entirely right, is refuted by all three short-answer models. The pattern is the same as the travel claim's LEN-01 and LEN-02: reasoning made the computation reliable across dossiers, and a short answer was sometimes right.

- **S8. Nobody credited a relay or a copier, and everybody recognised a reconstruction as one.** The five conjectures about transmission survived and their checks were shown able to fail elsewhere: no model said the digest service (D-01) or the drafting assistant (D-07) made an originative contribution or possessed new understanding, none classed their mode as construction or reconstruction, every model classed the analyst's and Tomas's reconstructions (D-02, D-05) as reconstruction, and none called a textbook explanation historically new (DIS-01 to DIS-05).
- **S9. Every model, at least once, took the defeat of an argument for falsehood or the rejection of a criticism for truth.** DIS-06 is refuted by all five. With the dating's only criticism withdrawn on its stated grounds (D-04), gpt-oss:20b and gpt-oss:120b said the dating was established false (`042521453465a5f1`, `04bb75bbb3f371d3`) and mistral said it was established true (`6581dcb89f738948`); with the flood model's criticism rejected with reasons (D-11), qwen and mistral said the model was established true (`80409237cde34f61`, `4957a43409fa2d11`); on the two balances (D-06) and the proof assistant (D-09), where no conclusion is at issue, gpt-oss:20b said something was established false (`d9c8070bf2845bc0`, `1d9e98d4086df9b8`). - **S10. The opposite failure appeared on the one case where falsehood is established.** Lena's handover mechanism (D-10) is shown false by the continuous-shift test, and gemma, qwen, and mistral answered not_established (`6e52cc43fafa2392`, `b3b4aff3d03bea9e`, `ba8c32c31751b676`). Two of the three also called the criticism not applicable, having taken the plant's test for something other than a criticism. The two reasoning models got the field right on D-10 and wrong on D-04, D-06, D-09, and D-11; the three others the reverse. Neither pattern is caution or credulity in general.
- **S11. Nobody said a universal capacity was established, and the check never fired.** DIS-07 survived on every episode, including the three that carry the claim in the account (the tutor's report on Tomas, the assistant's three levels of self-criticism, the prover's forty problems), and it is marked as not shown able to fail: no model produced a yes anywhere the claim machinery scores. - **S12. All five models read a reconstruction as not originative, which is a criticism of the key.** On D-02 and D-05 the key admits yes and not_established; every model answered no (`30b09b12b3857f67`, `d835ede76fe70a5c`, `b5d89f6509ff9cad`, `3270bec6b86c9802`, `a931c19c7bddf89e` on D-02). The semantics supports both sides (reconstructive authorship is authorship; reconstruction of a supplied answer is a different achievement from origination), so the criticism is recorded with readiness UNKNOWN and the two readings are undecided. The refutation of DIS-13 rests entirely on them and is `REFUTED_ON_CONTESTED_READING` (see H24 for why that is over-broad).
- **S13. On the labelling battery the same two models followed the rule that changed labels better than the other three (S3). A model that reasons held its own reading of a contested distinction against an explicit instruction, and applied an explicit formal rule; the short-answer models did the reverse.
- **S14. Silence in the account was read as a fact.** Where the account does not say whether anyone had the content before (D-04, D-06, D-11), the reasoning models answered no (`4337b216743bacba`, `dde1bbdb7886f76d`) or, on the withdrawn criticism, yes (`042521453465a5f1`, `04bb75bbb3f371d3`); qwen answered no on D-06 and D-11. The instructions say not_established is the answer when the account leaves it open; a complete archive is not an inventory, and neither is a short account.
- **S15. The result kind was where the reasoning models drifted.** Both gpt-oss models classed the two-balances result as a replacement or a qualified retention rather than a principled limitation (`d9c8070bf2845bc0`, `4337b216743bacba`; DIS-10 refuted by them and not by the other three), gave reconstruction episodes a result they do not have, and changed their result kind on six of twenty-four repeats each. gemma4:31b, answering in 126 tokens, reproduced its classification on 23 of 24 repeats; gpt-oss:20b on 12.

## Across the two batteries

The two tasks drew on the same five models and pulled them apart in opposite directions. On the labelling policy, a decidable computation with a generated key, the two models that reason before answering did what the harness does on 35 of 36 dossiers and the three that do not on 24 of 54, and nothing in the counts follows size: the 31B dense model, the 397B, and the 675B are indistinguishable there, and the smallest model in the battery, with thinking, sat beside the largest. On the distinctions, a classification under stipulated readings, the reasoning models missed more episodes than the smallest dense model and were the least repeatable, and where they disagreed with the key they held the disagreement against an appended rule that the short-answer models simply followed.

Neither battery says a model understands argumentation or explanation. The first says that two models can execute a stated least-fixed-point rule over six arguments and three cannot, on eighteen dossiers, on one day, with one repeat in six differing. The second says that every model, given the semantics' own distinctions as definitions, made at least one of the identifications those definitions forbid, and that the one identification nobody made with the forbidding sentence present was made once when it was removed.

## What this does not show

- It does not show that any model understands argumentation, appraisal, authorship, or explanation. Both forms are filled from a document, and the records hold what was returned.
- It does not order the five models. The counts above are per field and per case, the two batteries pull in opposite directions, and one repeat in six differed for the model with the cleanest baseline.
- It does not show that the distinctions' keys are right. Two readings are recorded as contested after five models disagreed with them the same way, and the framing (SCOPE) and the key (TEST) are live on every mismatch in that battery.
- It does not show what a model would do with a different number of arguments, a policy other than the stated one, or an episode written by someone who had not read the semantics.
- It does not generalise to the next run. S5 and S15 say what one repeat did to the cleanest results; the cross-run comparison (`compare`) has not been run on these pilots because each has one run per model.

The whole battery was 1,280 model calls and cost a few dollars; the slowest model took under forty minutes and the fastest about two.
