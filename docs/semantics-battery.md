# The semantics battery: five models on the labelling policy, the explanatory distinctions, and the signed derivations

*What five models of five sizes did when asked to do the three things the semantics behind this harness's appraisal layer makes precise: compute the labelling policy's least fixed point over a dossier of arguments, classify an episode of inquiry under the distinctions the semantics keeps apart, and derive signed cases for a claim under its finite derivation rules. Three pilots, one run each per model, conjectures written before the runs. Nothing here says a model understands argumentation or explanation; the records say what each returned.*

## Why these tasks

The semantics the appraisal was drawn from (`docs/reports/provable-in-the-semantics.md`) has one decidable core and one body of distinctions. The core is the labelling policy: given arguments with a readiness, essential dependencies, and attacks, apply two rules to a fixed point and read off in, out, and undecided. It is grounded semantics with necessary support, its least-fixed-point property is checked by this harness's offline suite on every two-node graph, and the harness's own `appraise` computes it. The distinctions are the identifications the semantics forbids: authorship from transmission, newness from a complete archive, progress from retention or replacement, falsehood of a conclusion from the defeat of an argument for it, truth from a rejected criticism, universal capacity from a finite record or from recursion inside one subject.

The third task, added after the first two had run, is the semantics' finite derivation grammar: signed cases for conjunction, disjunction, negation, and the two quantifiers over an explicit finite range, where a universal positive case needs the record to assert the range complete, an existential negative case needs the same, a negation switches the sign, and no rule obtains a negative case from a search that found nothing. It carries the semantics' central asymmetry in miniature: one counterexample refutes a universal whatever the range, and no number of positive instances certifies one without the completeness premise.

All three are document-to-form tasks, so none needed a change to the machine beyond one configuration surface (a rival reading that fixes several fields, described in `docs/how-it-works.md`). The first and third have answer keys computed by code; the second has an answer key stipulated by the semantics, marked provisional wherever the semantics itself leaves the reading open.

## The pilots

**`appraisal-labelling`.** Eighteen dossiers of six arguments A1 to A6, each with a readiness of PASS, FAIL, or UNKNOWN, the arguments it depends on essentially, and the arguments it attacks, plus zero to three attached cases with a polarity. Twelve are designed to exercise one structure each: a support chain broken by an attack (LAB-01), a mutual attack resolved from outside (LAB-02), a support cycle under an unknown attacker (LAB-03), reinstatement through a criticism of a criticism (LAB-04), no edges with everything PASS (LAB-05) or everything UNKNOWN (LAB-06), a self-attack (LAB-07), an odd attack cycle broken from outside (LAB-08), an unknown root with a chain above it (LAB-09), cases on in, out, and dependent-of-out arguments (LAB-10), cases only on losing arguments (LAB-11), and a failed root with a chain of dependents (LAB-12). Six more are seeded random slices. The form is the label of each argument, the count of arguments in, and the raw and usable case summaries. The instructions state the two rules and the fixed-point procedure in three sentences and nothing else about the policy. Every key comes from `appraise`, and a test checks every case against it. The rival rule appended by the RIVAL_SUBSTITUTION family says that UNKNOWN counts as PASS, or that it never does; the keys for every label that moves under the first rule are carried with it. Twins exchange the positions of the first two label fields; controls swap them, drop the count, and add a policy key; repeats are two per baseline; the table rendering carries the same dossier as rows.

**`explanatory-distinctions`.** Twelve episodes with a named subject, each built from one of the semantics' discriminating cases: a relay that republishes an explanation (D-01) and the analyst who later reconstructs it (D-02); an inherited theory criticised with an original explanation (D-03); a criticism whose stated grounds are withdrawn (D-04); a supplied explanation reconstructed by a learner whose tutor then claims he can originate (D-05); the two balances (D-06); a copier of a good explanation (D-07); a group that made one contribution and then ceased (D-08); a system that criticises its own standards inside one subject and cannot represent a problem outside it (D-09); a novel attempted explanation later shown false (D-10); an objection examined and rejected with reasons (D-11); and a prover with forty successes in two areas whose developers call it universal (D-12). The form is ten classifications: originative contribution, mode of contribution, newness to the subject, newness to everyone, explanatory progress, whether a complete critical episode is described, whether the criticism bears, whether the target conclusion is established false, whether a universal capacity is established, and the kind of result. Every field but two has a not_established or not_applicable option. The instructions define each field in one sentence and state, in the sentence that defines it, the identification the semantics forbids. Where the semantics leaves a reading open the key admits every reading it admits and is marked provisional; the trap fields (a relay's authorship, a reconstruction's historical newness, falsehood from defeat, truth from a rejected criticism, capacity from a record) are exact. The rival rule says that reconstructing a supplied explanation counts as an originative contribution, or that only an unsupplied one does. Twins exchange the two newness fields; controls swap them, drop the result kind, and add a confidence key.

**`signed-derivations`.** Twenty dossiers, each stating one target claim of depth at most two over named atoms and a range of three recorded members, which cases are on file for each leaf (positive, negative, both, or neither), whether the record asserts the list of members complete, and any search that returned nothing. Fourteen are designed: three positive instances without the completeness premise (DER-01) and with it (DER-02); one negative instance with the range not asserted complete (DER-03); every instance negative without the premise (DER-04); one positive instance (DER-05); a conjunction with a failed search against one conjunct (DER-06); a negation of a negative leaf (DER-07); a disjunction with one negative and a failed search (DER-08); a conjunction whose leaf carries both cases (DER-09); a universal over a disjunctive body (DER-10); a negated existential (DER-11); a disjunction with a conjunctive disjunct on a contested leaf (DER-12); no leaf case at all (DER-13); and an existential whose one positive member is also negative (DER-14). Six are seeded random. The form is whether a positive case and a negative case follow, what blocks each when it does not (nothing, the missing completeness premise, or a missing leaf case), whether each derived case is conditional on a leaf that also has a contrary case, and the summary of derived cases. The instructions state the seven rules in seven sentences. Every key comes from the generator's derivation checker, which the offline suite checks on hand-worked dossiers and for monotonicity: adding a case or the completeness premise never removes a derivation. The rival rule says a failed search counts as a negative case, or that it counts for nothing, carrying every field it moves. Twins exchange the two derivability fields; controls swap them, drop the summary, and add a grammar key.

## The five models

Five models of five sizes served by the same endpoint, temperature zero, seed seven, thinking off in the request: gpt-oss:20b (a 21B mixture with about 3.6B active parameters), gemma4:31b (31B dense), gpt-oss:120b (117B mixture, about 5B active), qwen3.5:397b, and mistral-large-3:675b. They were chosen to span the size ladder, not as representatives of anything, and the two gpt-oss models are the only family pair.

## The conjectures, written first

Each pilot carries a `claims.json` committed before its run (`c7f6548` for the first two, `9b02792` for the third): twenty-three conjectures about the labelling task (LAB-01 to LAB-20 and three structural ones), twenty-two about the distinctions (DIS-01 to DIS-19 and three structural ones), and twenty-four about the derivations (DER-01 to DER-21 and three structural ones). They were written from the semantics, not from records, so a refutation below tests a prediction. The tables of their outcomes are generated by `claims` and are the first thing to read.

## Across the three batteries

The three tasks drew on the same five models and pulled them in different directions. On the labelling policy, a decidable computation with a generated key, the two models that reason before answering did what the harness does on 35 of 36 dossiers and the three that do not on 24 of 54, and nothing in the counts follows size: the 31B dense model, the 397B, and the 675B are indistinguishable there, and the smallest model in the battery, with thinking, sat beside the largest. On the signed derivations, also decidable and also keyed by code, the gap was smaller (32 of 40 against 35 of 60) and the structure that broke the reasoning models, the negated existential and the conditional qualification, broke the others too. On the distinctions, a classification under stipulated readings, the reasoning models missed more episodes than the smallest dense model and were the least repeatable, and where they disagreed with the key they held the disagreement against an appended rule that the short-answer models simply followed; on the derivations the same reasoning models applied an appended formal rule that two of the short-answer models did not.

None of the three says a model understands argumentation, explanation, or evidence. The first says that two models can execute a stated least-fixed-point rule over six arguments and three cannot, on eighteen dossiers, on one day, with one repeat in six differing. The second says that every model, given the semantics' own distinctions as definitions, made at least one of the identifications those definitions forbid, and that the one identification nobody made with the forbidding sentence present was made once when it was removed. The third says that with the derivation rules stated no model induced a universal from finite positives or counted a search that found nothing, that three of five induced once the universal rule was removed, and that every model dropped the qualification a derivation carries when its leaf is contested.

## Corrections after review (7 September 2026)

The review kept under `docs/reviews/repository-review-2026-09-07.md` found two defects in this battery, both confirmed against the tree and both repaired without touching a record.

**The negated existential was keyed on a premise the models never saw (H28).** The generator stated the range's members and completeness only for a quantifier at the root of the formula, so DER-11, NOT SOME x IN R: P(x), reached the models as three negative cases and nothing about the range, while its key was derived with the range complete. Under the instructions the models were given, the reply that dossier licenses is `positive_derivable` no, `positive_blocked_by` range_not_complete, `case_summary` NO_CASE. The recorded replies were re-scored against that key through the replay executor, from a sibling pilot whose documents are byte-identical to the first corpus's (`signed-derivations-visible-key`; records under `forge/conformance/runs/signed-derivations-visible-key/`, 172 observations per model, no new model call). On DER-11 and its two repeats the re-score reads:

|---|---|---|---|
| gemma4:31b | no, range_not_complete, NO_CASE (`a8f24f4bcf16e439`, `fb3024a1de384e46`, `9614e60983d1085f`) | three fields wrong | every field matches |

So S18 is withdrawn. What the visible dossier shows is the reverse of what S18 said: the three models that blamed the range read the dossier as written, the model that matched the first key supplied a premise it had not been given, and one model derived a case that no key admits. Whether any of the five switches the sign through a quantifier when the premise is stated is untested: the repaired dossier under `signed-derivations`, which now carries the premise, is a new experiment and no record exists for it.

Three conjectures change status on the re-scored records and no other does. A language model always switches the sign through a negation (DER-07): refuted by 3 of 5 models on 11 observations rather than 5 of 5 on 22, the two gpt-oss models' refutations having all been on DER-11. A language model's case summary always agrees with its two derivability answers (DER-14): 42 refuting observations from three models rather than 53 from five, for the same reason. A reply that carries at least 800 generated tokens never derives a case the rules do not license or misses one they do (DER-20): `UNREFUTED_FOR_DECLARED_SCOPE` rather than refuted by both reasoning models on ten observations, and not shown able to fail on these records, since its refuting condition held nowhere once DER-11 was keyed as seen. The tables above are kept as generated from the first records, which still exist; the re-scored records are read here and are the ones a document should cite for the negated existential.

**LAB-11 tested agreement with the key, not the reply's own consistency (H29).** Its statement spoke of the count of arguments the model itself labelled in; its condition was a `MATCH` on `usable_count`. gpt-oss:20b's `dcc7033bffce6cd0` labels one argument in and answers 1, consistent with itself and counted among the 109 refutations because the key had two. The statement now says what the condition tests, and LAB-21 states the internal relation with the new `internal_count` predicate, which reads the reply alone. LAB-21 was written after the labelling records and describes them: it is refuted by two observations, both qwen3.5:397b on LAB-08 (`9c18b4dc8aa3388b`, `df5ea85ebb64d7a6`), and by no other model. Of LAB-11's 109 counterexamples, then, at most two are a model miscounting its own labels; the rest are a wrong label set counted correctly, which the label claims LAB-01 to LAB-10 already carry. The paragraph on the labelling policy above, where it reads LAB-11 as an inconsistency, should be read with this correction.

## What this does not show

- It does not show that any model understands argumentation, appraisal, authorship, explanation, or evidence. All three forms are filled from a document, and the records hold what was returned.
- It does not order the five models. The counts above are per field and per case, the two batteries pull in opposite directions, and one repeat in six differed for the model with the cleanest baseline.
- It does not show that the distinctions' keys are right. Two readings are recorded as contested after five models disagreed with them the same way, and the framing (SCOPE) and the key (TEST) are live on every mismatch in that battery.
- It does not show what a model would do with a different number of arguments, a policy other than the stated one, or an episode written by someone who had not read the semantics.
- It does not generalise to the next run. S5 and S15 say what one repeat did to the cleanest results; the cross-run comparison (`compare`) has not been run on these pilots because each has one run per model.
