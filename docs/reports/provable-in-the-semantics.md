# What is actually provable in the semantics

*A report on the semantic authority and staged specification (both version 1.1) from which this harness's appraisal layer was taken, written after the appraisal had been built, tested, and run over the travel-claim records. The two documents and their reference checker are not in this repository. Where this report says "the semantics" it means the pair of them; where it says "the harness" it means the code under `src/creib/forge/conformance/`.*

## The question, and the short answer

The semantics is about forty thousand words of philosophy of explanatory knowledge in the tradition of conjecture and criticism, a record model for keeping the history of an inquiry, a labelling policy for saying which arguments in a finite slice of that history are usable, and a list of semantic predicates (accounting for a problem, a reason bearing on a target, using a reason, authorship, newness, progress, capacity, universality) that the record is meant to carry evidence about without deciding. The question the maintainer asked is what in all of that is actually provable.

The short answer is that everything provable in it is about bookkeeping over finite records, and the documents say so themselves. There are about ten theorems, all of the kind a careful programmer proves about a data structure: an iteration terminates, two sets stay disjoint, an order is acyclic, a bit only ever flips one way. Each of them is true, each has a short proof, and the central one has now been checked independently in this repository by re-deriving it from the stated rules and enumerating every two-node case. Around those theorems sits a larger body of stipulation: choices about what the record should look like and which policy to run, which are not provable because they are decisions. And around that sits the substantive theory, which is not provable by construction: the semantics defines its predicates by interpretive obligations and refuses to give any of them a decision procedure, so nothing about a real system follows from the documents plus a record except under an interpretation that a person supplies and that another person can attack.

That is not a weakness discovered by this report. It is the design, and it is the same design as this harness's: the records refute universal statements about what a model did on a form, and they never touch whether the model understood the form. What the semantics adds is a vocabulary for keeping those two things apart, and one piece of machinery, the appraisal, for saying what a refutation rests on. The rest of this report sorts the statements in the documents into four kinds, states and proves the theorems, names the stipulations, and ends with a table of what a record can refute and what it can never reach.

## Four kinds of statement

Read with a pencil, every sentence in the semantics that asserts something falls into one of four kinds.

1. **Theorems about finite bookkeeping.** Statements about the record model and the labelling policy that follow from their definitions by ordinary induction. Provable, and proved below.
2. **Representation facts.** Statements that the record language can express some situation: mutual opposition between two arguments without a causal cycle, a creator that stops existing after its one contribution, a history recorded later than it happened. Provable by construction: write the record down.
3. **Stipulations.** Choices the documents make and label as choices: the labelling policy, the three-conjunct decomposition of universal explanatory disposition, the achievement-sensitive reading of creative inquiry, the event vocabulary, the four-tuple description of an attempted explanation. Not provable, because a decision is not a proposition; criticisable, which the documents invite.
4. **Substantive interpretations and their non-entailments.** The semantic predicates, each defined by what an adequate interpretation of it must explain, none given a decider. Nothing positive about a real system is provable from them. What is provable is a list of separations: that one predicate does not entail another, shown by describing a situation that satisfies the first and not the second under a stipulated interpretation.

The documents are unusual in marking which kind each of their claims is. The results table in the specification has a column for what each result does not establish, every bookkeeping report hard-codes the semantic decision to `NOT_EVALUATED`, and the verification report says in its second paragraph that the tests prove nothing about creativity, explanation quality, or universal capacity. The sorting below therefore mostly confirms the documents' own account of themselves, with a few places where a claim is weaker or stronger than its label.

## The theorems

### The labelling policy is the grounded labelling with necessary support, and it is the least fixed point

The policy the semantics calls DA-1 is stated over a finite set of arguments (the specification calls them applications), each with a readiness in {PASS, FAIL, UNKNOWN}, a set of essential arguments, and a set of arguments it attacks. Starting from two empty sets, apply simultaneously and repeatedly

```text
I' = I ∪ { x : ready(x) = PASS and essential(x) ⊆ I and attackers(x) ⊆ O }
O' = O ∪ { x : ready(x) = FAIL or essential(x) ∩ O ≠ ∅ or attackers(x) ∩ I ≠ ∅ }
```

until nothing changes; label I in, O out, the rest undecided. Five things are provable about it.

**Termination in at most 2|N| productive rounds.** Both sets only grow, both are bounded by N, and a round that changes nothing is the last; so at most |N| rounds add to I and at most |N| to O.

**Disjointness.** By induction on rounds. Suppose I and O are disjoint before a round. A node entering I has readiness PASS (so it does not enter O by the readiness clause), every essential in I (so none in O, by the hypothesis; so it does not enter O by the essential clause), and every attacker in O (so none in I; so it does not enter O by the attacker clause). A node entering O fails at least one of those three conditions, so it does not enter I. The simultaneous update therefore preserves disjointness. The harness raises if the invariant ever fails, and the offline suite has never seen it raise.

**Least fixed point.** Call a labelling (I, O) closed if it satisfies both rules as inclusions: every node the rules would put in I is in I, and likewise for O. The operator above is monotone on the finite lattice of pairs of subsets ordered by inclusion, so by the Knaster-Tarski theorem its least fixed point exists, equals its least closed labelling, and is reached by iterating from the bottom. This is exactly what the iteration computes. It was checked here without reference to the documents' tests: `test_labels_are_the_least_fixed_point_on_every_two_node_graph_and_on_sampled_larger_ones` re-derives the closure condition from the two rules as written, enumerates every two-node graph (four essential-edge sets, sixteen attack-edge sets including self-attack, nine readiness assignments: 576 cases) and 150 seeded random graphs of three or four nodes, and asserts for each that the iterative labelling is closed and is contained in every closed labelling. The documents' own enumeration counts 2,304 two-node cases because it admits an argument essential to itself; the harness's loader refuses that, since a reading that is its own premise is not a reading anyone can attack.

**Identity with grounded semantics.** With no essential edges and every readiness PASS, the two rules are the characteristic rules of the grounded labelling of abstract argumentation: a node is in when all its attackers are out, out when some attacker is in, and the grounded labelling is the least fixed point of that operator. The essential edges add necessary support in the usual way, a second requirement for in and a second route to out, and readiness adds a per-node floor: FAIL is out unconditionally and UNKNOWN can never be in. So DA-1 is the grounded labelling of a bipolar framework with necessary support and a three-valued readiness, and everything known about grounded semantics transfers: it is unique, it is sceptical (a node is in only when nothing could make it out), and a mutual attack with nothing outside it stays undecided.

**Locality.** Adding an argument that attacks nothing already present, is essential to nothing already present, and is attacked by nothing already present changes no existing label, because each node's membership in each round depends only on its own readiness, its own essentials, and its own attackers. This is what makes an appraisal file extensible: a new reading about a new case cannot disturb the standing of refutations elsewhere. The converse is also provable and worth stating: the labelling is not monotone in the argument set. Adding an attacker can move its target from in to out and its target's dependents from in to undecided or out. That is the point of an appraisal, not a defect in it, but it means a labelling is a fact about one file at one time and carries the file's digest for that reason.

Two corollaries the documents state and this analysis confirms. An in node has every essential node in, so withdrawing a premise (recomputing with it FAIL or attacked by an in node) removes its dependents from in; and a support cycle with no independently in entry stays undecided rather than lifting itself in. Both follow from the least fixed point: nothing enters I that the rules did not force.

### The record model

The record theorems are stated in the specification's results table and are provable by the same kind of argument.

- **External ancestry is acyclic.** Every non-initial artifact is created by exactly one entry, every parent reference names an initial artifact or one created by a causal predecessor, and the causal order on entries is a strict partial order. A cycle of parent edges among non-initial artifacts would give a cycle in that order.
- **A cut is inert.** A cut selects already-recorded entries; selecting them again creates nothing. This is a definition, and the theorem is that a report built from a cut is a recorder action, not a target occurrence.
- **A fully grounded packet extends any snapshot.** Fresh identifiers, the cut as causes, no incompatible transaction: one extended representation exists. The documents correctly say this proves nothing about whether any actor will produce the packet.
- **The raw presence summary is monotone.** Each polarity's bit flips from absent to present at most once. The usable summary is not monotone, since a later criticism can take a case's application from in to out; the documents say this too.
- **The contribution index guard.** Four claim keys that must share every index before the semantic predicates are consulted. Provable as a string comparison; the theorem is only that mismatched indices are refused before anything semantic runs.

This harness's records have the same shape of theorem, and they are checked in the same way. An observation's id is a digest of its canonical body, a run's id is a digest of its header fixed before any call, and a run's content digest is a digest of the whole record; so a record that replays to a different id is refused on load, a record file named for a different id than it holds is refused on load (H19 in the register), and a directory is read by enumeration so that loaded plus refused equals present. None of that says a record is true. It says which bytes a claim is about.

### What follows about the harness's own status logic

Two small theorems about the appraisal as wired into `claims` are worth writing down because they are the reason it is safe to run.

**The appraisal only weakens.** A conjecture is `REFUTED` when at least one refuting observation is usable, `REFUTED_ON_CONTESTED_READING` when none is usable and at least one is contested, and `UNREFUTED_FOR_DECLARED_SCOPE` otherwise. Without an appraisal every refuting observation is usable. So for every conjecture, the status with an appraisal is at most the status without, in the order REFUTED, REFUTED_ON_CONTESTED_READING, UNREFUTED, and no appraisal can create a refutation. An appraisal file is therefore a document a critic of the key can only use to withdraw refutations, never to manufacture them.

**Standing is a function of the footprint.** A refutation rests on a reading only if the reading supports a case and field, or a trigger, that the conjecture's condition inspects, on the corpus or pilot digest that scored the observation. The condition's footprint is computed from its syntax, so two conjectures with the same condition rest on the same readings on the same observation, and a conjecture that names no field rests on every argued reading the observation carries. This is what H18 fixed: before it, standing was computed from every criticised field on the observation, and a contested reading of the purpose field contested a refutation about a night count.

## The stipulations

The documents label these as choices. They are listed here because a reader who takes them for theorems will over-read the results.

- **DA-1 itself.** The specification says it "is a modeling choice, not the definition of rational appraisal." Other policies are allowed with declared semantics. The harness adopted it because it is the sceptical one: nothing is in that could still be put out.
- **UNKNOWN never becomes in by being unattacked.** A consequence of the rules, but the rules were chosen to have it. Its effect in this harness is that a reading whose criticism a person has not decided leaves every refutation resting on it contested until someone decides; a mutual attack can sit undecided indefinitely.
- **What counts as usable.** Here the harness and the documents differ, and the difference is a stipulation on each side. In the documents a case counts in the usable summary only when it is bound to an application labelled in; an unassessed case is reported separately and does not count. In the harness a refutation resting on no argued reading is usable. The reason is that the harness's readings are the answer key's, which a person wrote into the corpus before any model was called, so every refutation is already bound to a declared reading, and the appraisal records only the readings that have since been argued about. Absence of argument is not endorsement, and CLAUDE.md says so; but it is the harness's default, and a reader of the documents should know it is the opposite of theirs.
- **Readiness.** In the documents, local readiness is about a declared finite check route. In the harness it is a person's decision, recorded with a reason, that a reading is stated consistently and ready to stand. `readiness_reason` is a required field so that the decision is visible; nothing infers it from records. The verification report for the semantics makes the same point about its fixtures: activation assessments are stipulated.
- **The footprint rule.** That a refutation rests only on readings of the fields its conjecture names is the harness's choice, made after H18 showed the alternative was wrong. It is not in the documents, which have no notion of a conjecture over many observations.
- **The three-conjunct decomposition of universality, the achievement-sensitive reading of creative inquiry, the event vocabulary, and the four-tuple description of an attempted explanation.** All explicitly labelled as modelling proposals in the authority. None is used by the harness.

## The substantive theory, and why nothing positive is provable in it

The authority defines its predicates by obligations. An interpretation of "accounts for" must identify the explanatory work done by the commitments and distinguish it from associating an input with an output. An interpretation of "uses a reason" must explain how the reason participates in the response across a relevant contrast family. Authorship, newness, progress, and capacity are defined the same way, and the specification adds that a build "must not invent a Boolean implementation for a substantive predicate."

From this it follows, and the documents say, that no record entails any of them. What is provable is the list of non-entailments in the specification's second results table: sixteen separations, each shown by a described situation. Originating an attempted explanation does not entail creating knowledge (the attempt can be false). A critical episode does not entail authorship of its target (the target can be inherited). Receiving equivalent content does not entail absence of reconstructive authorship (a learner can reconstruct what was communicated). Current support does not entail truth. Constraint does not entail truth and falsehood does not entail constraint. Record completeness does not entail repertoire completeness. Graph reachability does not entail explanatory reach. Finite behavioural agreement does not settle universal capacity. Each of these is a theorem in the weak sense that the described situation is consistent with the definitions; each is only as strong as the stipulated interpretation under which it is described, which the documents say in the sentence after the table.

One of them deserves a line on its own because it is the one the harness lives on. **A finite record does not entail a universal capacity.** The authority proves it by exhibiting two models that agree on every recorded episode and differ at a problem the record does not cover. In plain logic this is the observation that no finite set of instances entails a universal statement over an open domain, and nothing about language models or creativity is needed to prove it. The harness's constitution, printed at the end of every report, is the same sentence: survival of criticism leaves a claim unrefuted; pass counts, consensus, and confidence do not justify it. The sweep of eighteen models over 147 variants is, in the semantics' own vocabulary, an observed-coverage report, and it is never a capacity case. The documents also state the useful converse: the lack of a deductive guarantee is not a prohibition on explanatory knowledge of universality. A person may still argue that a model has a capacity; the argument is an explanation of the model's organisation, not a count of its successes, and the records can only refute it.

## What was checked here, and how

- The documents' own verification was run: 66 tests, an enumeration of 2,304 two-node cases, nine detected mutations of the checker, all passing on Python 3.12 in under a second. That verifies the bookkeeping the documents ship, not the theory, which the documents say.
- DA-1 was re-derived from the two rules as stated in the specification and implemented in `appraisal.py` without copying the reference checker; the least-fixed-point property was then checked here by exhaustive enumeration and random sampling as described above, and the specific behaviours the documents list (mutual attack undecided, external defeater resolving it, support cycle not bootstrapping, criticism of a criticism reinstating the reading, unknown never in) are fixed by `test_labelling_policy`.
- The ten results and sixteen non-entailments were read and classified into the four kinds above. The arguments for the record theorems were checked by hand; the constructions in the authority's Part III were read as arguments and not machine-checked, and this report does not claim more for them than the documents do.
- Nothing was formalised in a proof assistant, here or in the documents. The documents' verification report says so; the audit that accompanies them lists a proof ledger that exceeded what was supplied as one of its findings.
- The harness's own record theorems are checked by its offline suite: content-addressing and no-clobber publication, refusal of a record whose id does not replay, refusal of a mis-named file, enumeration of a records directory, replay of committed records to their recorded ids.

## What a record can refute, and what it can never touch

The reviews kept under `docs/reviews/` put the general point as a certificate asymmetry: a counterexample is a finite object anyone can check, while "holds for every case" quantifies over an open class and has no finite witness. The semantics and the harness both build on that asymmetry. The table applies it to the kinds of claim that come up around this harness.

| Claim | What one record can do to it | What no record can do |
|---|---|---|
| "On this form, a model never emits a key outside the schema" (any `never` or `always` conjecture in `claims.json`) | Refute it: one observation whose condition holds, checkable by anyone with the record and the code | Confirm it; survival is for the records supplied |
| "This refutation rests on reading R" | Establish it: a bookkeeping fact from the footprint and the appraisal's support declarations | Nothing further; it is decided |
| "The check behind this survival can fail" | Witness it: one record anywhere on which the refuting condition held (H21) | Make an unwitnessed survival into a tested one |
| "Reading R of the key is stated consistently and ready to stand" | Nothing; readiness is a person's decision, recorded with its reason | Infer it |
| "Reading R of the key is correct" | Nothing; a record can show that every model disagreed with it, which is a reason for a person to look (H13), not a verdict | Decide it |
| "The model reasoned before answering" | Nothing beyond a proxy: a thinking channel was present, or the reply was long (LEN-01) | Establish that the tokens bore on the answer |
| "The model understood the document" (the semantics' accounting and reason-use predicates) | Nothing | Anything; the predicate has no decider by design |
| "The model can fill forms" (a capacity claim) | Refute a specific universal reading of it: one form it did not fill as stated | Establish it; the sweep is coverage, not capacity |
| "The endpoint enforces the schema it was sent" | Refute it: one extra or missing key (`format_enforced_by_server` false) | Establish it; the flag stays null |
| "The harness's suite holds this rule of the constitution" | Refute it: a mutation of the code that the suite does not catch | Certify it; the reviews' asymmetry applies to the harness as much as to any model |

The last row is the one the harness has not yet acted on. Its refusal sites, the `raise RecordError` statements that make it fail closed, have not been swept by deletion to see how many the suite reaches. `docs/reviews/README.md` records it as open work.

## Conclusion

What is provable in the semantics is its bookkeeping: that a finite labelling terminates, stays consistent, and is the least one the rules allow; that a record's ancestry is acyclic and its presence bits monotone; that a cut is inert; that an index guard is a string comparison. All of it is true and all of it was already claimed at exactly that strength by the documents themselves. What is stipulated is the policy and the vocabulary, which the documents label as choices and this harness adopted in part, with two defaults deliberately reversed. What is not provable is every substantive predicate the theory is about, because the theory defines them by what an explanation of them must do and forbids a decider, and the only theorems about them are separations.

For this harness the consequence is exact. A record refutes a universal statement about what a model did; an appraisal says which reading of the key that refutation rests on and whether a person has withdrawn it; a liveness witness says whether the check behind a survival was ever seen to fail. Nothing in the records, the appraisal, or the semantics says that a model understood, reasoned, or can. The semantics' contribution is to make it hard to write a sentence that pretends otherwise, and the appraisal is the forty lines of it that do work here.
