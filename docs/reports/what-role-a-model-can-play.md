# What role a language model can play in generation, given the evidence

*Written after the travel-claim sweep, the three rounds before it, the incident-form and leave-request runs, and the code reviews the same models were asked to perform. Every claim below that is about a model points at a conjecture id in `docs/what-the-records-refute.md`, a numbered finding in `docs/small-models.md` (T1 to T29), or an entry in `docs/failure-modes.md` (F, G, L, H). "Generation" here means producing a candidate: a filled form in these records, and by extension any artefact offered as a conjecture to a loop that criticises it.*

## The short answer

The records support one role and rule out two. A language model can be the generator in a conjecture-and-criticism loop: it produces a candidate for almost nothing, in seconds, in whatever shape it is asked for, and the candidate is worth exactly the criticism that is then applied to it. It cannot be the judge of its own or another model's candidate, because agreement between models is information about the test and not confirmation of the candidate, and because the records show that its verdicts, like its fills, come loose from the thing they are about in ways the model does not report. And it cannot be the source of the readings a refutation rests on: which answer the key admits, whether a criticism is ready to stand, what counts as stated. Those are decisions, and every place in this harness where a model's output touches one of them is a place where a person decided.

Two further things the records say about the generator role are less obvious. The request is part of the candidate: what a model generates is a function of the form, the instructions, and the sentences the harness appends, so much so that the presence of a `null` option was the difference between six models inventing a phone number and eighteen models declining to invent a date (F4 against ABST-01). And the model's own citations are receipts it writes for itself, which are worth something only because a check outside the model reads them against the document (PROV-01, PROV-02). The role, stated carefully, is generator and receipt-writer under an external critic, with the request under test alongside the model.

## What the records say the generator does

**It produces the shape it is asked for, whether or not it can produce the content.** Every model returned a JSON object, or something the parser could recover one from, on nearly every call: twelve of eighteen wrapped or fenced it (STRUCT-03), two repeated keys (STRUCT-04), and the endpoint's format constraint did not stop any of that (STRUCT-10). A well-formed object is the cheapest thing a model makes; the reviews kept under `docs/reviews/` report the extreme case, where a decoding constraint guaranteed a parseable reply while making the underlying work unreachable. In these records the shape was never the problem and never the evidence.

**Given an honest exit it takes it; given none it invents.** On the case where the document states neither a return date nor a night count, all eighteen models returned `null` for both, in the baseline, in the repeats, and in the boundary variant (ABST-01, ABST-02, unrefuted over the sweep). On the incident form, which offered no such option, six of nine models invented a phone number where none was stated (F4). Where the form had two optional fields most documents do not mention, six models wrote placeholder values into them (STRUCT-07), and three of the six were the three sizes of one family (T22). The records cannot say that models are honest when allowed to be; they say that on these two forms the option was the difference (L3), which is a fact about form design. The reviews report the same direction from other settings: a fallback clause letting a model declare a task impossible cut specification gaming to a few percent, and a deployed agent fabricated only once every honest exit had been sealed.

**Reading was mostly right at this difficulty; arithmetic and derivation were where it broke.** No model took the copied-in colleague as the approver, a transit city as the destination, or a double negation the wrong way (READ-02, READ-03, READ-05, unrefuted), and T27 says why that is a weak survival: those probes were at the floor of what these models can do and criticised nobody. The multiplied line item was the battery's most reliable criticism: fifteen of eighteen models got at least one total wrong on the cases where a nightly rate had to be multiplied and the phrasing was unambiguous (ARITH-02, T23), six summed a foreign-currency face value as dollars (ARITH-03), and eight, at least once, counted the nights between two dates they had themselves stated correctly and got the count wrong (DERIV-04). Six still returned `null` for a date the document fixes by a weekday after the instructions defined that as stated (DERIV-01), and defining it moved two models from abstaining to deriving it wrongly about half the time (T10).

**Instructions bind most models most of the time, and not the same few.** An appended rule fixing how a numeric date is read was ignored by six models (RULE-01), a rule on which of two conflicting totals to claim by three (RULE-02), a rule on how to count nights by four (RULE-03); two models of one family are in every list. An inverted formatting instruction changed every model's output on every variant (RULE-04), which is a survival the liveness column marks as never having been seen to fail. "As named in the document" had no shared meaning across models on the incident form (L5).

**Citations are receipts the model writes for itself.** Asked to quote the words each value came from, every model did so for every value (PROV-03), and every name copied "exactly as written" lay inside the words quoted for it (PROV-04). But six models cited words that do not occur in the document (PROV-01): a misspelt name, a range with a different separator, a date the document gives only as an interval, and once a place that does not exist (`Hobitat` as the source of `Hobart`, T15). Twelve models completed a date range into a full date the document never writes (PROV-02), which the battery decided to accept as a quotation and records as accepted by a relaxation rather than verbatim (H4). The receipt says where the model claims the value came from; the harness's check says whether the words are there; neither says the value is right. The reviews put the general rule as: evidence for a claimant's assertion cannot remain under the claimant's sole control.

**A fixed seed is not repeatability.** A byte-identical request returned different form values at least once for sixteen of eighteen models (STAB-01), and the noise floor ranged from zero differing repeats in eighteen for gemma4:31b to fourteen for nemotron-3-nano (T14). Within a family, size did not buy repeatability: the three nemotron sizes reproduced their output 8, 5, and 6 times in 18 (T17). Across runs, the same request returned the same form on 104 of 117 shared requests for one model and 12 of 108 for another (T24). Anything that compares one call with another, which is most of the harness's families, inherits that floor, and the REPEAT family exists so that a reader can subtract it (H3).

**The models whose arithmetic held were the ones that reasoned before answering, in whichever channel they had.** Thirteen models generated between 230 and 310 tokens per reply, the JSON and nothing else; five generated far more, three in a thinking channel the request had asked them not to use and two as prose before the object (T26, T28). Those five are the five with no wrong total or one in the sweep, and for the smallest of them that is a fact about one run: on the same requests eight hours earlier it got five totals wrong (T29). The conjecture that makes this checkable, that no reply of 800 tokens or more got an unambiguous multiplied total wrong, is unrefuted (LEN-01); its converse, that short replies never get one right, is refuted by six models (LEN-02), so the reasoning was reliably sufficient here and not necessary. It did not protect the derivable date (LEN-03, refuted by five models that reasoned and still returned `null`), and it costs five to fifteen times the tokens and three to fifteen times the time. One deliberation of 15,934 tokens ended in a refusal (H22), the only one in every record of every pilot, and the harness's refusal detector missed it because the model wrote its apostrophes as typographic quotes.

**Size predicted little; version and family predicted more.** Within one family, size bought fewer errors of each kind but not repeatability (T17); one tier step removed a flash tier's habits entirely (T18); one version step did what no size step did, taking a family from thirty-odd wrong totals to one and from twelve identical repeats to eighteen (T19); code tuning changed nothing this battery measures (T20); the smallest model that thinks in a channel matched the largest on arithmetic and paid in time (T28). On the incident form the 20B and 30B models appeared in some failure modes the 397B and 675B did not, and the reverse (L9).

## The role that evidence supports

**Generator of conjectures under criticism.** The synthesis in `docs/reviews/synthesis-verifier-kernels.md` names the cost asymmetry that makes this the right role: production is nearly free and verification is the expensive, unreliable side. Everything the harness does is an attempt to restore the asymmetry the other way, to make the candidate expensive to fake and cheap to check: content-addressed records that replay to their ids, controls that show the oracle can reject a corrupted answer before any model is called, receipts read against the document, a noise floor measured within the run, checks that must be shown able to fail. Inside that machinery a model is a good generator precisely because it is cheap and fast and because its failures, on this evidence, are legible: the wrong total is on the multiplied line, the invented value is a placeholder, the missed rule is the same rule for the same family. A generator whose failures cluster is a generator whose output can be criticised efficiently.

**Not the judge.** The constitution says agreement between models never confirms, and the records give the reason in one incident. When all four round-one models read "two nights at $205.00" as a total against a key that read it as a nightly rate, the identical failure across four models of three families was a criticism of the key, not four refutations of four models (T2, H13). Agreement was information about the test. The reviews add the mechanism: resampling one model gives near-clones, cross-family correlation is still substantial, and no aggregation rule downstream can extract information the signal does not carry; the same reviews' negative ladder found most plausible ways of using a model to pick the right candidate made things worse. In this harness a model's verdict about a candidate, its own or another's, has no place to go except into the record as one more conjecture.

**As a critic, a source of conjectures with a target and a bearing claim, whose readiness a person decides.** Before the pilots, five of the same models were asked to review the harness's own code. They produced ninety-two findings in fifty-two distinct places. A few named a line and a consequence and were confirmed by reading the code and became regression tests; many restated intended behaviour as a defect, or objected to a line that did what the finding said it should. That experience is why the appraisal treats every criticism the same way whoever wrote it: an argument with a target, a stated defect, and a readiness of `UNKNOWN` until a person marks it ready with a reason. A model-authored objection is never labelled in by default, and the attack graph itself is written by a person, because, as the reviews put it, argumentation semantics cannot repair an unvalidated attack relation. Under that discipline a model is a useful critic in the way a model is a useful generator: it is cheap, it finds some things, and what it finds is a conjecture.

**As a receipt-writer, under an external reader.** The spans are the harness's small version of the receipt discipline the reviews describe for terminating agents: a claim is bound to a citation, and a check the claimant does not control reads the citation. The records show why the second half matters. Every model cited; six cited words that are not there; twelve cited a date they had constructed. Without the check, the citations would have looked like provenance. With it, they are evidence of exactly what the check can see, and the kernel table in `docs/how-it-works.md` says what it cannot: a quotation of the wrong sentence that does occur in the document is `GROUNDED`.

**With the request under test.** AUXILIARY is live on every failure after a model call, and the records justify it. The abstention sentence the harness appends invited a companion key the schema does not define, and one model emitted it forty-seven times until the sentence was reworded (H14). Defining "stated" changed what two models did with a derivable date (T3, T10). The empty replies appeared when the prompt grew (T11). Which prompt a model was given is as much a part of the candidate as the model, and a loop that criticises the candidate criticises the request with it.

## What the evidence does not support

- **Any capacity claim.** Eighteen models on nineteen documents and one form is, in the semantics' vocabulary, an observed-coverage report and never a capacity case (`docs/reports/provable-in-the-semantics.md`). "This model can fill forms" is refuted by a form it did not fill as stated and confirmed by nothing.
- **A size ordering.** The records do not support one and the harness does not compute one (L9, T17 to T20).
- **A causal claim about reasoning.** LEN-01 is a survival of a conditional stated with a proxy: 800 tokens stands for "reasoned", and the record holds a token count, not what the tokens said. The kernel table lists that blind spot. A battery with harder arithmetic and a way to switch reasoning on and off for the same model would test the conditional directly; nothing here does.
- **That any model understood the document.** No record touches the semantic predicates, by construction.
- **That the survivals were tested.** Nine of the fifteen surviving conjectures name a condition that held on no travel-claim record at all (H21). Five of those name detectors that have never fired on a live record of any pilot, and one of the five, the refusal detector, was shown by inspection to have missed the one refusal there was (H22). A survival of a check that was never seen to fail is a fact about what the models did, not a test the harness passed.
- **That the conjectures were predictions.** They were written after rounds one to three had been read. The refutations demonstrate the machinery; the next battery commits its conjectures before its run.

## What follows for anyone using a model to generate

1. **Give it an exit.** A form or task that admits "the document does not say" got that answer from eighteen models; one that did not got inventions from six of nine. The exit has to be typed and it has to cost nothing to take.
2. **Ask for receipts, and read them outside the model.** A citation the generator writes is a claim; the check that reads it against the source is the evidence. Decide in advance what counts as a fair quotation (H4) and record which relaxation accepted each one.
3. **Send every request more than once and count.** Temperature zero and a fixed seed gave sixteen of eighteen models at least one differing repeat. Anything compared across calls needs the floor measured in the same run.
4. **Keep the request under test.** A changed sentence in the prompt changed what the models did as often as a changed model did. Record the request digest with every reply.
5. **Never let a visible score select.** Agreement between candidates, or between models, is information about the test. The reviews' two anti-patterns, sealing honest exits and letting a visible score promote, are the two things the records most clearly show to matter.
6. **Treat a model's criticism as a conjecture with unknown readiness.** Record it, name what it attacks, and let a person mark it ready or not; never let it defeat anything by being entered.
7. **Show every check able to fail, and pre-register what would count as failure.** A survival whose refuting condition never held anywhere is not a survival of anything; a conjecture written after the records is a description.
8. **Expect the failures to cluster, and build the next battery where they did not.** The arithmetic probes were at the ceiling and the reading probes at the floor (T27); the evidence about the generator is only as good as the probes that discriminated.

## What this report rests on

All of it is refutable and most of it is narrow. The records are one form family, nineteen short English documents, one day, one hosted endpoint, eighteen models chosen by what that endpoint served. The claim statuses are those of `docs/what-the-records-refute.md` at the time of writing, with the corrections that document itself makes to its table (XPRT-01 is refuted by inspection; nine survivals are not shown able to fail). The three readings that rest on argued readings of the key (DERIV-04, READ-07, PROV-01) are marked contested in part, and the round-one arithmetic readings the key's author withdrew support nothing. The code-review experience is a recollection of a session, recorded in the harness's early history and not in a record the claims machinery can read; it is the weakest evidence in this report and is cited only for the discipline it motivated. Everything else points at an observation id, and the observation ids point at bytes.
