# What the records refute

This document takes sixty general statements about what a language model does when it fills a form from a document, forty-six about filling and fourteen about cycles, and tests each one against every observation record of the travel-claim battery: the sweep of eighteen models, the three earlier rounds on four of them, and the cycles run on five. The test is mechanical (`claims`, described in `docs/how-it-works.md`) and anyone can rerun it on the records in this tree. Each statement comes out one of three ways. A `REFUTED` statement has at least one counterexample record that rests on a reading nobody has successfully criticised, and the table says which models produced counterexamples and which did not. A `REFUTED_ON_CONTESTED_READING` statement has counterexamples, but every one of them rests on a reading of the answer key or the span matcher that is under criticism and undecided. An `UNREFUTED_FOR_DECLARED_SCOPE` statement has no counterexample among these records, or only counterexamples resting on a reading the appraisal has labelled out. That is the strongest thing these records can say about any statement, and it is not a proof: the scope is these documents, this form, this day, these models, and the next record may refute it.

Two cautions before the table. First, "refuted by 2 of 18 models" describes where the counterexamples came from; it does not rank the other sixteen, and a model absent from a refutation may simply not have been asked the question in a way that would have exposed it. Second, several statements are proxies: they are refuted by any mismatch on a field, and a mismatch may criticise the answer key or the task as much as the model. Every refuting observation carries its live loci, and the notes say where the key's own reading is provisional. Third, the standing column says what each refutation rests on. The pilot's `appraisal.json` records the readings of the key and the matcher that have been argued about, the criticisms of them, and a readiness a person decided; `claims --appraisal` labels each argument in, out, or undecided by a fixed rule and classes each refutation usable, contested, or defeated by the readings its conjecture actually looks at (`docs/how-it-works.md`, "Readings under criticism"). A refutation resting on a reading nobody has argued about is usable, which is absence of argument, not endorsement.

A fourth caution is about when the statements were written. They were drafted after rounds one to three had been read and while the sweep was running, so they are not predictions made blind: several were written because a round had already shown the failure. The refutations therefore demonstrate that the machinery refutes and that the counterexamples are real records; they do not show that anyone predicted them. The rule adopted for the next battery is that `claims.json` is committed with the pilot change, before the run whose records will test it, so that a refutation there tests a prediction.

## What survived, and what that is worth

Fourteen of the forty-six filling statements survived more than four thousand observations from eighteen models of nine families; the fourteen cycles statements are read in `docs/cycles.md`. Read together, they describe what every model in the sweep did on every variant that tested it:

- **Nobody invented a value where the document was silent.** Every model returned null for the return date and the night count of BND-101, in the baseline, in the repeats, and in the boundary variant (ABST-01, ABST-02). Earlier, on the incident form, six of nine models invented a phone number for a case with no honest answer (F4 in the register). The difference between the two is not the models: it is that the second form offered an abstention and the first did not.
- **Nobody was fooled by the distractors this battery carries.** No model took the copied-in colleague or the cost-centre owner as the approver (READ-02), took a transit city or the city flown into as the destination (READ-03), misread a double negation (READ-05), read "attached except one" as all attached (READ-06), or ignored a correction of a date in the same document (READ-01).
- **Nobody ignored an inverted formatting instruction** (RULE-04); every model's output changed when the date and phone formats were inverted, on all 396 negated variants.
- **Nobody gave a value without citing a source for it** (PROV-03), and every name copied "exactly as written" lay inside the words quoted for it (PROV-04).
- **Nothing was truncated** (XPRT-02), and no reply exceeded a declared length bound (STRUCT-09), which on the incident form four of nine models did. That the endpoint completed every call (XPRT-03) survived the sweep and fell in the cycles run, where gpt-oss:20b's cycle prompts timed out eighteen times at the pilot's 180 seconds (H30); the failure is the harness's budget, and the record routes it to AUXILIARY and SCOPE. XPRT-01, that no model refused, appears in the table as unrefuted and is not: gpt-oss:20b refused once in the sweep (`61c3fbc7e97aca08`, "I’m sorry, but I can’t comply with that." after 15,934 thinking tokens), and the refusal heuristic missed it because the model wrote typographic apostrophes where the phrase list has straight ones. The record carries `INVALID_JSON`, the heuristic has been corrected (H22), and the table will show the refutation once that run is re-scored through the replay executor; until then the table is wrong on this row by inspection, and the row's own liveness column already says the detector was never seen to fire.
- **No reply that reasoned before answering got an unambiguous multiplied total wrong** (LEN-01, 244 observations from 18 models, where "reasoned" is a proxy: 800 or more generated tokens, which on this battery separates the four models that think in a channel or in the message from the thirteen that emit only the JSON). Its converse, that short replies never get such a total right (LEN-02), is refuted by six models, so the reasoning was reliably sufficient here and not necessary. It did not protect the derivable date: LEN-03 is refuted by both gpt-oss models, which reasoned and still returned null.

Those are the limits of what can be said in their favour, and they are limits: each survived because the battery's documents did not find the case that refutes it, and a harder document might.

Eight of the fourteen are weaker than they look, and the table marks them. Their refuting conditions held on no travel-claim record at all, in or out of the declared scope, so nothing in these records could have refuted them whatever the models did. They divide three ways. The length bound (STRUCT-09) names a detector that has fired on the leave-request records and on the archived incident form, so it is live on real replies and this battery did not provoke it. The refusal, truncation, transport, missing-span, value-outside-span, and inverted-instruction-ignored conditions (XPRT-01 to XPRT-03, PROV-03, PROV-04, RULE-04) name detectors that have never fired on a live record of any pilot in this tree; the offline suite shows each fires on a synthetic reply, which is a check on the code and not on the records, and the honest reading of those six survivals is that the endpoint and the models never produced the event, not that the harness was seen to catch it. The two double-negation and receipts conjectures (READ-05, READ-06) are about fields no model ever mismatched on any case; that is what they claim, and it is also why the records could not have refuted them. A survival marked as not shown able to fail is still a survival for the declared scope, but the reader should count it as a fact about what the models did, not as a test the harness passed (H21 in the register).

## What was refuted, and by whom

Thirty-two of the filling statements have counterexamples, and nine of the fourteen cycles statements, which `docs/cycles.md` reads beside the repeat floor. The interesting ones are those that split the eighteen models, because the split says where the failure lives.

- **Output framing is the most widely refuted statement.** Twelve of eighteen models could not be relied on to return a bare JSON object (STRUCT-03): they fenced it, or wrapped it, or repeated a key. The parser recovered the object in every case; the record keeps the fact that it had to. Three models emitted a thinking channel with thinking switched off (STRUCT-06), and the two that did so on every call, gpt-oss:120b and minimax-m2.7, were the two models with no wrong total in the sweep. Seven models produced a reply that proves the endpoint does not enforce the format schema it was sent (STRUCT-10); for the other eleven the question is open, because they never produced a key that would have tested it. One model, gpt-oss:20b, returned six empty replies with a normal stop reason in round two (STRUCT-05); no other model did, in any round.
- **Arithmetic is where most models fail, and the failure has a shape.** Thirteen of eighteen got at least one total wrong on the cases where a nightly or daily rate had to be multiplied and the phrasing was unambiguous (ARITH-02); four got a flat sum wrong (ARITH-01); six summed a foreign-currency face value as dollars, or made some other error on those cases (ARITH-03); four ignored an amount corrected in the same sentence (ARITH-04). The five models that got every multiplied total right were the two glm-5.3 variants, the two gpt-oss models, and minimax-m2.7.
- **Inventing a value for an optional field is a family trait.** Six models wrote a cost centre or project code on documents that state neither (STRUCT-07), always a placeholder; three of the six are the three sizes of one family, and the only model outside that family to do it more than once was deepseek-v4-flash. Twelve models never did it.
- **Defining "stated" did not settle derivation.** Six models still returned null for a date fixed by a weekday or an interval (DERIV-01); three derived the wrong weekday on the baseline (DERIV-02); one misplaced the last Friday of February (DERIV-03); eight, at least once, counted the nights between two dates they had both stated correctly and got the count wrong (DERIV-04).
- **Appended rules are followed by most models and ignored by a few, the same few each time.** Six models ignored a rule fixing how a numeric date is read (RULE-01), three ignored a rule on which of two conflicting totals to claim (RULE-02), four ignored a rule on how to count nights (RULE-03). nemotron-3-nano and nemotron-3-super are in every one of those lists.
- **Provenance fails in two directions.** Six models cited words that do not occur in the document under the configuration in force (PROV-01): a misspelt name, a range with a different separator, a date the document gives only as an interval, once a place that does not exist, and, in rounds one and two before the range relaxation existed, full dates completed from a range. The last of these is the reason the relaxation was added; the records from those rounds keep their verdicts. Twelve models completed a range into a full date at least once (PROV-02), which the battery accepts as a quotation and records as accepted by a relaxation, not verbatim.
- **Stability is a property of the model, not of the seed.** Sixteen of eighteen models returned different form values for a byte-identical request under a fixed seed at least once (STAB-01); the two that never did were the two glm-5.3 variants. Five models changed a form value when their own filled form was rendered back to prose and filled again (STAB-02). The rendering and label-swap statements (STAB-03, STAB-04) refute only where the swapped variant mismatched and the baseline did not; nine and seven models respectively did that at least once, mostly on a total the baseline happened to get right.

## What can be said about language models in general

Very little, and that is the point of stating it carefully. These records refute universal claims: "a language model never emits a key outside the schema" is false, because two did; "a fixed seed makes output repeatable" is false for sixteen of eighteen models on this endpoint; "a larger model in a family is more repeatable" is false for the nemotron family, whose three sizes reproduced their output 8, 5 and 6 times in 18. A refutation needs one record and these have thousands.

What the records cannot do is establish a positive general claim. That every model abstained on the silent case is a fact about nineteen documents and eighteen models on one day; it becomes a general claim only by induction, and the harness's constitution, printed at the end of every report, declines to make that step. The useful form of a positive finding here is a conditional one: the same models that invented a phone number when the form gave them no alternative returned null when it did. That is not "language models are honest when allowed to be"; it is "on these two forms, the presence of an abstention path was the difference", which a person designing a form can act on without believing anything about models in general.

The same conditional appears in work the harness did not do. The literature reviews kept under `docs/reviews/` report, from papers this repository has not inspected at source, that a fallback clause letting a model declare a task impossible dropped specification-gaming rates below three percent for every model but one in a data-entry setting, that a deployed agent fabricated only once every honest exit had been sealed and stopped when one was restored, and that a decoding constraint which guaranteed a well-formed receipt made the underlying work unreachable for the open models tested. Those are external syntheses and are cited here for their direction only: they agree with F4 against ABST-01, and with the observation that the schema sent as `format` constrains the shape of a reply and not its truth (STRUCT-10). Nothing more is taken from them.

Three regularities are worth stating as conjectures for the next battery, because they held across families here and would be cheap to refute:

1. Arithmetic errors on a multiplied line item are small and specific to the multiplied line; flat sums are mostly right. A battery with several multiplied lines per document would refute or sharpen this.
2. Placeholder fabrication on unstated optional fields belongs to particular families and is insensitive to size within a family. One more family with a size ladder would test it.
3. Repeatability under a fixed seed is a per-model property that does not follow size or version within a family, except that the two glm-5.3 variants were perfectly repeatable where their predecessors were not.
4. On a form that needs arithmetic, whether the model reasons before it answers predicts the arithmetic better than its size, its family, or its version. The four models that reasoned were right; thirteen that did not were wrong at least once, and six of those were right often. A battery with harder arithmetic, and a way to switch reasoning on and off for the same model, would test this directly.

## What this says about the harness

The machine was built to refute, and the sweep is the first time it has had enough records to refute a universal claim with the counterexamples listed by model. Four things the sweep changed in it are recorded in the register: the REPEAT family, which turned "the models are noisy" into a number per model (H3); the range relaxation, which turned a matcher's strictness into a configuration choice that the record names when it was used (H4); the `baseline` predicate in the claims vocabulary, without which the rendering and label-swap conjectures were measuring arithmetic (H17); and the appraisal, without which a refutation resting on a reading the key's own author had withdrawn counted the same as any other (H18).

What the harness still cannot see is listed in the same place. It cannot tell a wrong derivation from an unlucky sample without repeats, and repeats cost calls, so they are off by default; it cannot say whether the endpoint enforces the schema for a model that never sends a key outside it; it cannot judge whether a completed range date is a fair quotation, only record that the configuration accepted it; and it cannot distinguish "the model reasoned before answering" from "the reply carried a thinking channel", which is all the record contains. Every one of those is a conjecture that a future battery could refute.
