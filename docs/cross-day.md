# The same requests seven hours apart: a first cross-day floor

*Written after two runs of the travel-claim baselines and repeats on gemma4:31b and mistral-large-3:675b, sent in the same shuffled order (seed 7) at 23:43Z on 8 September 2026 and at 06:31Z on 9 September, seven hours apart, the second while three cycles runs shared the endpoint. Records: `forge/conformance/runs/travel-claim-v3-order/` (runs `ab3aad863db2cb4d` gemma4:31b, `556e3baac1b73553` mistral-large-3:675b) and `forge/conformance/runs/travel-claim-v3-order-day2/` (runs `da1e2ca4287be51b`, `9c71ccc06c9092da`). Every observation carries `started_at` and `elapsed_ms`, the first records to. The comparison is generated by `compare`, which pairs byte-identical requests by digest and repeat index and decides identity on the form's fields without the oracle; no conjecture was written before the second run, so what follows is a description of the records, not a test of a prediction.*

## What `compare` found

| model | shared requests | identical | differing | field that differed | verdict moves | repeats differing within day 1 | within day 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gemma4:31b | 27 | 22 | 5 | `total_claimed_cents` (5) | none | 3 of 18 | 3 of 18 |
| mistral-large-3:675b | 27 | 24 | 3 | `total_claimed_cents` (3) | none | 1 of 18 | 3 of 18 |

Per-call timing, by the harness's clock around each request:

| model | day | calls | elapsed, min / median / max (ms) | sent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gemma4:31b | 8 Sept | 27 | 1,051 / 1,358 / 5,576 | 23:43:27Z to 23:44:13Z, alone with mistral |
| gemma4:31b | 9 Sept | 27 | 1,098 / 2,372 / 14,710 | 06:31:49Z to 06:33:25Z, beside four other runs |
| mistral-large-3:675b | 8 Sept | 27 | 2,946 / 3,303 / 4,976 | 23:43:27Z to 23:44:56Z |
| mistral-large-3:675b | 9 Sept | 27 | 2,976 / 3,367 / 4,016 | 06:31:49Z to 06:33:17Z |

No call failed on either day.

## Reading

- **Drift across the seven hours is confined to the arithmetic field and never crossed the key.** Every request whose reply differed between the days differed on `total_claimed_cents` and on nothing else, and no field verdict moved: each drifted total was wrong on both days, in two different ways. The drifted requests are the multiplied-total cases (TRV-001, TRV-001-P, TRV-004 for gemma; TRV-001 and TRV-001-P for mistral), the same cases whose repeats differ within a run.
- **A request sent again seven hours later differs about as often as one sent again within the minute.** gemma4:31b: 5 of 27 across the days against 3 of 18 within each day; mistral-large-3:675b: 3 of 27 against 1 of 18 and then 3 of 18. On these cases the cross-day difference is not distinguished from the repeat floor; it is the same field moving in the same way.
- **The drift did not follow the load.** gemma4:31b's day-2 calls ran up to 14.7 seconds where day 1's ran up to 5.6, with its own cycles run and three others sharing the endpoint; of its five drifted replies, one came from a 14-second call and four from calls of 1 to 3 seconds, and the other 14-second call returned the day-1 form. mistral's calls took the same time on both days. This is the first thing the timing fields have shown: that a slow day was slow, and that the slowness was not where the drift was. Without `elapsed_ms` the second sentence could not have been written.
- **The shuffled order contributed nothing here, by design of the comparison.** Both days used seed 7, so every request had the same position in both runs; the order removed a confound between position and case only within a day. A second day on a different seed would separate position from case across days, and is the run to make next if position is ever suspected.

## What this does not show

Two models, twenty-seven requests, seven hours, one endpoint. It does not show that drift stays inside the floor for other fields, other models, or longer intervals, and it cannot: a run on a third day can refute it with one reply. It says nothing about why the totals move, and the harness has no field for that. The statement it would support as a conjecture for the next run is "a byte-identical request sent on another day never returns a different value on a field whose repeats never differ"; written before that run and committed with it, it can be tested; written here, after these records, it is only what they show.
