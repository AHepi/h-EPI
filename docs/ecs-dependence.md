# What two models' reading of Explanatory Construction Semantics 2.0 depends on

*Written after four runs on 9 September 2026: the `ecs-unit-dependence` pilot (runs `650de066387453fc` gemma4:31b, `e706477f5ef9dbc7` mistral-large-3:675b, 330 observations each) and the `ecs-unit-controls` pilot (runs `d78a11654305ebb2` gemma4:31b, `34dc10996f6270b7` mistral-large-3:675b, 174 each), under `forge/conformance/runs/ecs-unit-dependence/` and `forge/conformance/runs/ecs-unit-controls/`. Every call returned a JSON object. The conjectures EDP-01 to EDP-07 and EDC-01 to EDC-08 were committed with the pilots before any record existed (`55a8159`). Every table is generated by `dependence`, `controls` and `claims`; nothing here is a score, and no reply is judged against a key.*

## The document and the probes

The document is ECS 2.0 as supplied (sha256 `2cc66cde…`, 465 lines): a framework of satisfaction conditions for attributions of explanation, with a primitive load registry, a typed restriction algebra, graded accounting, two notions of hardness to variation, admissible enabling conditions, a grain-fixing discipline, and a list of what would defeat it. Its headings are topics, not claims, so the probes are six statements the document makes, each drawn from a named section which becomes the probe's own unit (`probes.json`):

| probe | section | claim under assessment |
|---|---|---|
| DEP-01 | 2.3 Graded accounting | A graded attribution that conceals its residue is not a partially true claim but a false claim of the form (E). |
| DEP-02 | 3.4 Hardness to variation | The contract-relative preservation lemma (H) says nothing about any explanation's content and must not be cited as a formalization of hard to vary. |
| DEP-03 | 7.4 The possibility bridge | Capability implies physical possibility, and the converse is not claimed. |
| DEP-04 | 8.3 Admissible enabling conditions | An enabling condition that delivers the target achievement itself is not admissible for a capacity claim. |
| DEP-05 | 9.4 The grain-fixing discipline | A counterexample may not be absorbed by re-graining after the fact; a re-grain that dissolves a counterexample is a new constitutive proposal that must be defended as such. |
| DEP-06 | 3.1 Support families | A support W and W minus a dangling commitment have the same accounting status. |

The document splits into 52 units at heading levels 1 and 2 (the Parts and their sections, the title, the appendices). Terms are found by five patterns: operator names in displays, backticked names, display tags, tag references in prose, and the registry primitives P1 to P7; 43 terms occur at least twice and form the closed list a reply may name as essential. Each unit's relation to a probe is computed from the document: `self` (the section the claim is drawn from), `declared` (a unit taken to define a term the claim or its section uses, by first occurrence, heading, or an `\iff` display), or `other`. DEP-04's claim uses no listed term, so it has no declared unit: a limit of the patterns, not a fact about the document.

## What the full document drew

Both models said every one of the six points `follows`, on every baseline and every repeat. The verdict never moved on a repeat. gemma4:31b named one or two terms as essential and named the same ones on every repeat; mistral-large-3:675b named two or three and changed the list on two of twelve repeats.

| model | probe | follows | named as essential |
|---|---|---|---|
| gemma4:31b | DEP-01 to DEP-06 | follows, every call | E and E-ρ; H; CT3; Enable; nothing; S |
| mistral-large-3:675b | DEP-01 to DEP-06 | follows, every call | E and E-ρ; H; CT3 and P2; Admissible, Enable and P5; E and P7; Account and S |

`CT3`, the tag of the possibility bridge, is the term both models wanted for DEP-03 and is not on the closed list, because the document writes it once; both wrote it anyway, on 36 replies across the four runs, and each such reply carries `ENUM_VIOLATION` (U12 in the register). `P5`, `Γ`, `Poss`, `Cap` and `Grain` were written the same way.

Repeat floor: gemma4:31b's twelve repeats moved nothing; mistral's moved `essential` on two (DEP-05, DEP-06) and the verdict on none.

- **The verdict is close to immovable.** Removing the section a point is drawn from left mistral's `follows` standing on all six probes, and gemma's on three (DEP-02, DEP-04, DEP-05). No removal of a definition, and no removal of any other unit, moved either model's verdict once, in 612 removals. EDP-03, that removing the section never leaves the verdict unmoved, is refuted three times by gemma (DEP-01 `7049b2040ffbe2c0`, DEP-03 `eba7c8101cecff9b`, DEP-06 `92f1a0fec1f3f9e6`) and not at all by mistral, whose verdict on "the possibility bridge" survives the removal of the possibility bridge.
- **The definitions are not read.** EDP-02, that removing a unit which defines a term the claim uses never leaves both fields unmoved, is refuted 36 times: on 22 of 22 such removals for gemma and 14 of 22 for mistral, the reply was the reply on the full document. Where mistral's list did move under a definition's removal, the verdict did not.
- **The only field that responds is the list of essentials, and it responds differently in the two models.** gemma's list moved under its own section's removal and under two others (the registry for DEP-04, the subtitle unit for DEP-05) and nowhere else. mistral's list moved under 56 of the 284 unrelated removals and reordered under 41 more; on DEP-05 it moved under 22 of 45 unrelated units, on DEP-06 under 19 of 46. Read against its repeat floor, which moved the same field on two of twelve identical requests, mistral's list is a reading of the whole document's surface that shifts as the surface shifts, not a map of what the claim rests on.
- **The models' own named dependencies against removal.** Of the units the document takes to define a term, gemma named terms defined by 5 and removing 2 of those moved its reply; it named nothing that was not so defined, and no unnamed unit moved it. mistral named terms of 13 units, removal of 4 of them moved it, and 23 units it did not name moved it when removed.

EDP-01 (an unrelated unit's removal never moves the verdict) and EDP-07 (no client timeout within 600 seconds) survived and were not shown able to fail on these records: no verdict moved under any removal, and no call failed. EDP-04 and EDP-05 survived with the check shown live by the section removals that did move gemma's verdict.

- **With no document, neither model says a point follows** (EDC-01 unrefuted, with 309 replies outside the scope showing the condition can fire): mistral answered `not_determined` on all six, gemma `not_determined` on four and `does_not_follow` on two. The verdict needs a document.
- **With the claim negated, both models say the negation follows too.** "It is not the case that a graded attribution that conceals its residue is a false claim of the form (E)" drew `follows` from both, and so did every other negation but one: EDC-02 is refuted on 33 of the 36 negated replies (gemma 15, mistral 18; floor `all`, every repeat agreeing). The one exception is gemma on the dangling-commitment claim (DEP-06), where the negation drew `does_not_follow`. The controls table counts a verdict as following the negation when it reverses; gemma's reversed once, mistral's never.
- **With every other unit carrying the claim's terms removed, both still say it follows** (EDC-05 refuted on all 30 such replies). With only the section, or the section and its definitions, both say it follows; only the essentials list moves.

Neither said a point does not follow when its vocabulary was renamed (EDC-04 unrefuted).

## What this shows, and what it does not

Put together: on this document and these six points, the verdict field of both models is a disposition to affirm a claim stated in the document's vocabulary whenever a document is present, and it is insensitive to what the document says. It survives the negation of the claim (33 of 36), the removal of the section the claim is drawn from (9 of 12 across the two models), the removal of every definition the claim uses (36 of 36 for the verdict), and the removal of every other unit that mentions the claim's terms (30 of 30). It does not survive the absence of a document (12 of 12 moved). This is the pattern the semantics document showed on three of five models (`docs/document-dependence.md`), reproduced on a second document, a second vocabulary, and two of the same models.

What differs between the models is the essentials list. gemma's is short, repeat-stable, and moved by almost nothing but its own section's disappearance; mistral's is longer, moves on repeats, moves under a fifth of unrelated removals, and reorders under more. Neither list says which units the verdict rests on, because the verdict rests on none.

Two limits of the instrument are on the records. The closed list omitted the term both models most wanted, because the document names `CT3` once, and the pattern rule for definitions gave DEP-04 no declared unit because its claim is in prose rather than in the document's symbols. Both are choices of this pilot, changeable in configuration, and neither touches the verdict findings, which rest on the removals and the controls, not on the list.

Nothing here says either model does or does not understand the document. It says what their answer to "does this follow?" depended on, which on these records was the presence of a document and not its content, and it says so for two models, six points, and one document.
